The story behind the work

The challenge

Faculty and students need more than a blanket statement about AI. Expectations vary by assignment, and instructors need a way to explain the learning purpose behind those choices.

The approach

I lead MCC’s AI Task Force and have helped develop a stoplight framework that lets instructors identify activities as Red, Yellow, or Green for AI use. The model is intended to make expectations visible and connect them to what students are meant to learn.

Putting guidance into practice

My faculty development work includes AI in the Classroom, prompt writing, introductory sessions for employees, and tool showcases. The AI in the Classroom course asks participants to apply the framework to an assignment, discuss tradeoffs, and redesign with clearer expectations.

Leadership lens

The work combines policy language, teaching practice, and campus conversation. It calls for listening to faculty concerns, building shared vocabulary, and revisiting guidance as tools and classroom uses evolve.

A note on evidence

Framework and task force work are institutional collaborations. This page describes my leadership and contributions without implying sole authorship.
